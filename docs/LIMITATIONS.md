# Limitations

Honest limitations, written for the paper's limitations section.

- **Scale.** The engine is a CPU, float64, single-threaded-per-worker TypeScript implementation intended for networks with at most tens of thousands of parameters. It is a teaching instrument, not a training framework.
- **Provenance of the prototype.** The pre-existing prototype described in project documents was never committed; no claims about it can be verified (see `docs/AUDIT.md`).
- **Instrument.** The concept inventory is researcher-developed and, at the time of writing, unvalidated beyond internal consistency; gains are therefore reported by four concept clusters — fundamentals (7 items), optimisation (5), backpropagation (5) and convolutional (3) — and by Bloom level (recall 4, understand 9, apply 7), with per-concept results only as an exploratory appendix, because most single concepts rest on one to three items. Two items whose content no condition teaches (overfitting, symmetric initialisation) were retired before data collection (ADR-032).
- **Test selection.** Shapiro–Wilk is highly sensitive at larger samples, so the pre-specified rule may select the non-parametric primary test for trivial departures from normality; both analyses are always reported.
- **Comparison condition.** TensorFlow Playground cannot show convolutional networks or discrete steps, so the C2 condition cannot cover the CNN content with the same medium. It also cannot teach the MSE formula (CI09), the numerical chain rule (CI13) or the softmax gradient (TR02); see `docs/INVENTORY-COVERAGE.md`.
- **Accessibility.** The tool is designed to WCAG 2.1 AA. It has been checked with an automated audit (axe-core, zero violations on 47 screens: the 19 workbench and Study Mode screens; every novice screen in both themes, before and after a prediction with every "going deeper" item open, 26 more (Fix Round 2); and the parameter editor and the bound inspector, 2 more (Fix Round 2b)) and a manual criterion-by-criterion review, but not with screen-reader users or other assistive-technology users. Automated tools detect only a subset of WCAG failures, so AA conformance is not claimed; learners who rely on assistive technology may meet barriers the audit did not find.
- **Novice path, partly built (Fix Round 2).** By the 30 October review, three of the ten experiments (1, 5, 6) have a purpose-built beginner screen. The other seven show the workbench, filtered by each experiment's declared surface. The redesign follows human testing with the team, not with the target learners; whether it reduces load and raises gains for first-time learners is for the pilot and the study to show.
- **C1/C3 parity until C1 is regenerated.** C1's static pages are still generated from the workbench walkthroughs. They must be regenerated from the novice screens, with coverage parity re-verified, before any participant session (STUDY-PROTOCOL §5a).
- **Intervention timing is estimated, not measured.** The per-experiment durations in STUDY-PROTOCOL §5a come from a reading-and-actions model; the pilot must measure them.
- **Predictions are a secondary, uncalibrated measure.** Prediction accuracy early and late in the session (telemetry) depends on which predictions a participant reached. Predictions are not inventory items and are reported descriptively only.
- **The NaN/∞ breakpoint cannot be reached from the shipped controls.** No configuration a learner can set produces a non-finite value; the breakpoint is a safety net, verified by unit test on a constructed divergent run (`docs/CONTROL-AUDIT.md`, finding 5).
